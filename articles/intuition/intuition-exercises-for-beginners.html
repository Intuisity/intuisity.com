Intuition Exercises for Beginners

The easiest way to begin is to practice with short, specific exercises that give you a chance to notice, choose, and learn from feedback.

Beginner intuition exercises should feel simple enough to actually do. If the practice is too complicated, you will start analyzing instead of noticing. A good exercise gives you one clear moment to pause, sense, choose, and compare.

1. The first impression exercise

Before opening a message, answering a call, or choosing between two ordinary options, pause and write down your first calm impression. Then continue normally and compare what happened later.

2. The hidden picture exercise

Choose which color, card, or square feels like it is hiding an image. Do not change the answer repeatedly. In Intuisity, Train Your Knowing gives this exercise immediate feedback.

3. The person reading exercise

Look at a portrait without reading the name. Choose the personality traits or life details that feel most true. Then reveal the person and compare your impressions.

4. The remote viewing sketch

Before seeing a target image, draw shapes, textures, lines, or colors that come to mind. The point is not artistic skill. The point is learning whether your first visual impressions carry useful information.

5. The daily action prompt

Choose one real-world action, such as calling the first friend who comes to mind or writing a short reflection. Later, check whether the action gave you information, connection, or clarity.

How to Make Exercises Work Better

For each exercise, keep the pressure low. Intuition is easier to observe when you are not trying to prove anything. A calm practice lets you notice what your signal feels like: quick, quiet, visual, physical, emotional, or simply known.

Track patterns instead of judging one attempt. A single result can be luck, distraction, or a genuine hit. Repeated practice teaches you which impressions deserve trust and which ones are usually mental noise.
